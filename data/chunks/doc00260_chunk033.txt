first attempt at a mechanical derivation, contained in a " supplement " dated 13 january 1822, assumed that ( i ) there were three mutually perpendicular directions in which a displacement produced a reaction in the same direction, ( ii ) the reaction was otherwise a linear function of the displacement, and ( iii ) the radius of the surface in any direction was the square root of the component, in that direction, of the reaction to a unit displacement in that direction. the last assumption recognized the requirement that if a wave was to maintain a fixed direction of propagation and a fixed direction of vibration, the reaction must not be outside the plane of those two directions. in the same supplement, fresnel considered how he might find, for the biaxial case, the secondary wavefront that expands from the origin in unit time — that is, the surface that reduces to huygens ' s sphere and spheroid in the uniaxial case. he noted that this " wave surface " ( surface de l ' onde ) is tangential to all possible plane wavefronts that could have crossed the origin one unit of time ago, and he listed the mathematical conditions that it must satisfy. but he doubted the feasibility of deriving the surface from those conditions. in a " second supplement ", fresnel eventually exploited two related facts : ( i ) the " wave surface " was also the ray -
